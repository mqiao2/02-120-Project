Assumes surface water is in equilibrium with the air and total alkalinity is fixed at 2300 µmol/kg, a typical open-ocean value. In the real ocean alkalinity rises and falls with salinity, so treat low-salinity settings (below about 30) as what-ifs. Not for estuaries or coastal water. Estimates only: published equilibrium constants disagree by about 0.01 pH (up to 0.05 in the coldest and warmest water) and by a few percent in Ω.

Ω (omega) is the aragonite saturation state. Below 1, seawater is corrosive to aragonite, the mineral in shells such as those of pteropods. Above 1, aragonite does not dissolve, but shell-building organisms are already stressed as Ω falls.
